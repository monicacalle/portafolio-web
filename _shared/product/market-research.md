# Positioning & Market Research — Mónica Calle Portfolio

> Purpose: decide how to label and word this portfolio so it competes in Mónica's
> actual job market. Findings come from a multi-source, adversarially-verified
> deep-research pass (21 sources fetched, 25 claims verified — 18 confirmed, 7
> refuted). Each finding notes a **confidence** level and its sources. This doc
> is the reference for the later copy/narrative work — it does **not** change the
> site by itself.
>
> Last updated: 2026-07-24.

## 1. Who Mónica is (the profile we're positioning)

- **Junior / early-career**, career changer (background in foreign languages).
- **Based in Valencia, Spain**; targets Valencia + **remote/hybrid Spain** and the broader remote‑EU market.
- **Hybrid skill set**: UX/UI + graphic design **and** front-end development — Figma, Adobe CC; HTML, CSS, JavaScript, React, WordPress, Webflow, GSAP.
- **Currently self-labels** "Diseñadora UX/UI & Front-End".
- **Actively applying to "Product Designer" roles** at Spanish tech companies (Huspy, Shakers, Redbility, Vidext).
- Salary band ~**€30–46k**; bilingual (Spanish primary, English B2).
- Stretching on "N years of experience" requirements — carried by the portfolio.

The tension this research resolves: her **title says one thing (UX/UI + Front-End)** while her **applications target another (Product Designer)**, and her strongest *technical* differentiator (design + code) maps to a *third* label (Design Engineer).

## 2. The market she's entering (2026)

**The junior design market is highly competitive and AI has changed the bar.** _(Confidence: HIGH)_

Nielsen Norman Group's *State of UX in 2026* is explicit: "the supply of aspiring UX professionals will still outpace open roles, **especially at the junior level**," and entry-level positions "remain scarce and highly competitive." Crucially on _what now differentiates_:

> "If you're just slapping together components from a design system, you're already replaceable by AI. What isn't easy to automate? Curated taste, research-informed contextual understanding, critical thinking, and careful judgment."

**Implication for Mónica:** she is exactly the cohort hit hardest, so the portfolio cannot lead with UI screens. It must foreground **research, decisions, and problem‑solving** — the things AI can't do. Pretty screens are table stakes; thinking is the differentiator.

Source: [NN/g — State of UX 2026](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/state-of-ux-2026/) (primary, Jan 2026).

## 3. The label decision — the core question

### Recommendation: **lead with "Product Designer" (Diseñadora de Producto); keep "UX/UI" as a secondary keyword; treat design + code as a differentiating *angle*, not a job title.**

### 3.1 Why "Product Designer" over "UX/UI Designer" _(Confidence: HIGH on the definition; MEDIUM on "more senior / market-standard")_

- **It's the local convention on a board she targets.** A real posting — **Mercadona Tech** (a major Valencia employer) — advertised a hybrid UX/UI/visual role titled **"Product Designer" at €38–42k** (inside her band) on **GetManfred**, with **no "UX/UI Designer" variant**. It defines the role as product/PM‑adjacent: _"el product designer no solo se limita a pintar pantallas… está muy cerca de lo que hace un Product Manager: analiza el mercado, analiza el negocio, analiza a los usuarios."_ → [posting](https://www.getmanfred.com/ofertas-empleo/2907/mercadona-tech-product-designer-feb24)
- **It's named a top in-demand role for 2026** because "teams are getting smaller and more cross-functional, and products are growing more complex." → [UX Design Institute](https://www.uxdesigninstitute.com/blog/the-ux-job-market-in-2026-2/)
- **It's the broader remit** — UX/UI **plus** product strategy, market research and business-goal alignment — and is described as the "more business-oriented" role; moving UX → Product is framed as "a natural transition as you gain more experience." → [Coursera](https://www.coursera.org/articles/product-designer-vs-ux-designer-the-difference-explained), [Seven Peaks](https://sevenpeakssoftware.com/blog/wil-product-designers-replace-ux-ui-designers-in-2030)

**Keep "UX/UI" present** (meta description, skills, keywords) so recruiters and ATS searching either term still find her. This is a discoverability hedge, not the headline.

> ⚠️ Nuance the adversarial pass flagged: two sources hold that "Product Designer" and "UX/UI Designer" are often used **interchangeably at all levels** in practice. So treat the "more senior evolution" framing as *positioning*, not fact — she should still apply to both titles.

### 3.2 Why NOT to headline "Design Engineer / UX Engineer" _(Confidence: MEDIUM)_

Her Figma + React/HTML/CSS/GSAP stack is a **1:1 match** for the "Design Engineer" category — "the intersection of visual design and front-end development," end‑to‑end concept‑to‑shipped ownership. The role "became widely recognized in 2024" (Notion, Vercel, Linear, Clerk).

**But**: those examples are **all US firms**; sources concede US pay ($120k–$250k+) with "other markets (Europe, Asia) still developing," and the title is essentially **absent from Spanish boards** (which are dominated by "UX/UI Designer" / "Diseñador de Producto"), where UI-designer pay is a fraction of US figures.

→ [jux.io](https://blog.jux.io/p/the-design-engineer-a-new-career), [uxdesign.cc](https://uxdesign.cc/the-design-engineer-symptom-what-a-rising-job-title-reveals-850d5e4fd9cc), [Lee Munroe](https://leemunroe.medium.com/the-rise-of-the-design-engineer-d428e7d681fd)

**Conclusion:** the code fluency is a **superpower to foreground as an angle**, worded descriptively (e.g. "diseña y lleva a código", "del prototipo en Figma al front-end en React") — **not** adopted as a standalone job title that would narrow her to a near-empty Spanish niche.

> ⚠️ Refuted claims (do **not** rely on these): "Design Engineer talent pool is unsaturated → easy junior entry" (killed 0-3); "hybrid roles are rare in EU so the label narrows options" (killed 0-3); "Design Engineer = UX Engineer are interchangeable" (killed 1-2 — keep them distinct). Net: the EU picture is genuinely murky, which is itself a reason to hedge with a mainstream title.

## 4. Portfolio copy — what converts in 2026

### 4.1 Hero / tagline: **specific, not clever** _(Confidence: MEDIUM)_

- "Specific beats creative — name your specialty." ("UX designer for fintech" > "passionate designer"; "the best taglines weren't clever — they were clear and specific.") → [TheCrit](https://thecrit.co/resources/portfolio-tagline-examples)
- "A simple statement of what your name is and what you do is fine… avoid **unicorn, ninja, wizard**." → [Dribbble junior guide](https://dribbble.com/resources/career/junior-ux-designer-portfolio)

**Apply:** plain name + concrete specialty + her differentiator, e.g.
> "Mónica Calle — Diseñadora de Producto que diseña y lleva a código (Figma → React) · Valencia"

(Her current copy is already clean — no clichés — so this is a sharpening, not a rescue.)

> ⚠️ The adversarial pass **refuted** one source's rigid tagline formula "[Skill/Role] + [Unique Angle]" (killed 1-2). Use the *principle* (specific + differentiator), not a fixed template.

### 4.2 Case studies: **few, deep, thinking-first** _(Confidence: MEDIUM)_

From UXfolio's 2026 Playbook (aggregating 74 UX recruiters) and UX Design Institute:

- **3–5 projects at most, presented in detail** — "I'd rather see one project explained really well than five explained quickly." (Candidates are judged by their **weakest** project.)
- Every case-study intro should do five things: **describe the product · explain her role/responsibilities · introduce the target audience · reveal the main challenges · highlight project limitations.**
- "Show **how you think**, not just what you design… explain the problems you solved, the research that informed your decisions, and the impact your work had."

→ [UXfolio Playbook](https://blog.uxfol.io/ux-portfolio-playbook/), [UX Design Institute](https://www.uxdesigninstitute.com/blog/the-ux-job-market-in-2026-2/)

**Apply:** the current Projects section is a card → PDF/external-link gallery. The highest-leverage change is to turn **Vibe** and **Voluntee** (her UX projects) into 2 **on-page** case studies with the 5-part intro, foregrounding research + decisions. This is content work, scheduled after the i18n refactor.

### 4.3 Framing the career change / thin experience _(Confidence: MEDIUM)_

> "A portfolio is **not a retrospective of your career. It's a proof of how you think today**." "Starting with limited experience isn't disqualifying." → [UXfolio Playbook](https://blog.uxfol.io/ux-portfolio-playbook/)

**Apply:** she should **not** apologize for thin tenure or hide the languages background. Self-initiated and freelance projects, presented as rigorous **current** problem-solving, are legitimate anchors.

> ⚠️ Refuted: the "recruiters spend 3–5 minutes" figure (killed 1-2); other sources say **under 1 minute** / a "10-second test." Either way the lesson holds: the hero and each case-study intro must land the value **fast**.

## 5. Language of the site (Spanish / English / bilingual)

**Status: an open question the research could not settle.** No surviving claim resolved it. Signals: the local primary posting (Mercadona/GetManfred) was in Spanish, **but** GetManfred/LinkedIn frequently post in **English** even for Spanish companies, and remote-EU roles + ATS keywords often favor English.

**Working decision (inference, not a verified finding):** make the site **bilingual (ES default, EN toggle)**. This is why i18n is being installed now — it keeps her strong in the Valencia market while opening remote-EU. Note "**Product Designer**" reads correctly untranslated in both languages, so the headline label survives either way.

## 6. What the research left open (verify before over-committing)

1. **Site language** — ES vs EN vs bilingual (see §5). Bilingual is the hedge.
2. **Hard posting counts** — no head-to-head volume for "Product Designer" vs "UX/UI Designer" vs "Diseñador/a de Producto Digital" on InfoJobs / Tecnoempleo / GetManfred. Worth a 10-minute manual board check before fully committing the label.
3. **Code angle for pure PD roles** — for Huspy / Shakers / Redbility / Vidext specifically, does foregrounding front-end read as a **plus** or as "non-specialist"? Untested. The hero wording should be able to dial this up or down.
4. **The word "junior"** — including vs omitting it in the headline: effect on Spanish recruiter/ATS response is unknown.
